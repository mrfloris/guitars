## Why It Belongs In The Archive

The sitar has a different place in the collection from an instrument chosen in a shop. Its importance begins with where it came from and the connection it preserves. A fuller public story can be added after the instrument and the family history around it have been reviewed together.

## Identification Plan

A careful identification should begin with the instrument itself rather than a similar-looking sitar found online. The next record should include:

- Clear photographs of the full front and back.
- Close-ups of any label, stamp, signature, handwriting, or maker's mark.
- The body, neck, tuning pegs, frets, bridges, strings, and decorative work.
- Overall dimensions and the number and arrangement of strings.
- The case and any accessories or notes that came with it.
- Visible wear, cracks, loose parts, repairs, or replaced components.

Those observations can then be compared with reliable maker or specialist references. Until that work is complete, this page makes no claim about the sitar's origin, age, model, tuning, or readiness to play.

## Still To Document

- Maker, workshop, or region of origin, with the evidence used for the identification.
- Approximate age and how it was established.
- Materials, construction, string layout, tuning, and playing setup.
- Condition, previous repairs, maintenance needs, and safe storage.
- When and how my father acquired it, how he used it, and what it meant to him.
- A public-safe photo set checked for private labels, names, addresses, reflections, and location clues.
